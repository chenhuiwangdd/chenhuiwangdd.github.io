Research

Econometric methods for understanding extreme events — in financial markets and in the climate.

Tail risk and dependence among extremes

My work develops methods for characterizing tail risk and the dependence structure among extreme observations, with particular attention to how this behaviour varies across units in large panels rather than assuming a single shared tail.

Heterogeneity in extreme value behaviour

A central theme is heterogeneity: extreme value indices that differ across a panel, and tail behaviour that shifts over time. Capturing this heterogeneity is central to getting risk measurement right when the units being studied do not all behave the same way in their tails.

Applications: finance and climate

I study extreme value indices in large panels, time-varying tail behaviour, and the persistence of extreme temperatures — applying tools from extreme value theory to measure risks arising from both financial and climate-related events.

Keywords: extreme value theory, tail risk, extreme value index, panel data, time-varying tails, climate extremes.
